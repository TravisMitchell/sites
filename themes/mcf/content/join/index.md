## Who we look for

The group works on high-fidelity simulation of multiphase, non-Newtonian and particle-laden flows, flow in porous and fractured media, heat transfer and clean energy processes (see [Research](/research/)). We welcome students and researchers with backgrounds in mechanical, chemical, civil, mining or petroleum engineering, physics, applied mathematics or computer science who enjoy combining physics, numerical methods and programming.

## PhD

PhD candidates in the group undertake an independent research project, typically developing or extending numerical models and applying them to an engineering problem. Projects may involve collaboration with industry partners or other research groups.

1. **Contact us first.** Email a short enquiry (see [what to include](#what-to-include-in-your-enquiry)) so we can discuss possible projects and supervision before you apply.
2. **Check eligibility and apply** through the [UQ Graduate School](https://graduate-school.uq.edu.au/), which explains entry requirements, application rounds and the application process.
3. **Scholarships.** Information on research scholarships for domestic and international candidates is available from [UQ Scholarships](https://scholarships.uq.edu.au/). Project-specific scholarships, when available, are listed under [current opportunities](#current-opportunities).

## Honours, masters and visiting researchers

- **Honours and masters students** at UQ interested in a computational thesis project are welcome to contact us to discuss topics related to our research themes.
- **Visiting students and researchers** who would like to spend time with the group, for example during a PhD or on sabbatical, should email us with their proposed dates, research topic and source of funding.

## What to include in your enquiry

To help us respond quickly, please include in your email to [t.mitchell@uq.edu.au](mailto:t.mitchell@uq.edu.au):

- the position you are interested in (PhD, masters, honours or visiting) and your preferred start date;
- a short CV, including your degrees, grades and any publications;
- academic transcripts (unofficial copies are fine at this stage);
- a few sentences on which of our [research themes](/research/) interest you and why;
- your experience with numerical modelling and programming (and links to code, a thesis or reports if available);
- for international applicants, your English-language test results if you have them.

## Skills that help

You do not need to tick every box, but the following are valuable in our group:

- **numerical modelling** and a strong grounding in fluid mechanics and applied mathematics;
- **computational fluid dynamics** (CFD), whether with commercial, open-source or in-house codes;
- the **lattice Boltzmann method**, or related mesoscopic and particle methods such as the discrete element method;
- **high-performance computing**, including GPU computing and working on clusters;
- **programming**, for example C/C++, CUDA, Python or MATLAB, and version control with Git.

Experience with lattice Boltzmann techniques and high-performance computing is a benefit but is not essential: enthusiasm for computational fluid dynamics and a willingness to learn matter most.
