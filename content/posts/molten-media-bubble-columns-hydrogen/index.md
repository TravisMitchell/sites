Methane pyrolysis in a molten media bubble column reactor is a promising route to hydrogen production with low carbon dioxide emissions. Because molten media are opaque, the bubble dynamics inside these reactors are difficult to observe experimentally. In this study, a volume-of-fluid CFD model, first validated against existing experimental and empirical observations, was used to examine how the gas injection rate and the properties of the gas and the molten medium (iron, aluminium and a sodium bromide-potassium bromide salt mixture) affect the bubbles.

The results showed that gas properties have only a small effect: bubble diameters for argon at ambient temperature and at 1600 °C differed by less than 10%. In contrast, the volumetric gas injection rate and the properties of the molten medium significantly affected the bubble diameter and flow regime. The authors note that future work will build on these findings to recommend operating conditions and molten media for specific pyrolysis reactor designs.

**Authors:** R. Okajima, [T.R. Mitchell](/people/t-mitchell/), [C.R. Leonardi](/people/c-leonardi/) and S. Smart

**Published in** *Physics of Fluids* (2024). DOI: [10.1063/5.0227299](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0227299)
