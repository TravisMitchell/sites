The movement of mining equipment makes methane dispersion in underground longwall mines difficult to predict and manage. This computational study used transient CFD to investigate how shearer operation affects airflow, methane and oxygen dispersion. Dynamic and overset meshing techniques were integrated to model both the translational movement of the shearer (0.5 m/s) and the rotation of its cutting drums (60 RPM), and the model was validated against existing experimental data. Unlike earlier studies that assumed a uniform methane flow from the mining face, methane release was modelled as peaking in front of the moving shearer and shifting along the face as it advanced.

The results show that the moving shearer and its counter-rotating drums create significant turbulence that alters airflow around the cutting face and tailgate. After 80 s, the average gas flow velocity at the tailgate increased from 3.59 m/s (non-operating) to about 5.78 m/s as the shearer advanced, with the average methane concentration at the tailgate reaching 2.4%. The authors highlight the relevance of these findings to mine ventilation design and real-time methane measurement.

**Authors:** S. Sadeghi, S.M. Aminossadati, M. Kizil and [C. Leonardi](/people/c-leonardi/)

**Published in** *Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology* (2025). DOI: [10.1016/j.tust.2025.106500](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tust.2025.106500)
