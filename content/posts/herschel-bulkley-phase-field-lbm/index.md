Non-Newtonian fluids, and their interaction with other fluid phases, matter in a wide range of science and engineering problems. This study presents the development and validation of a three-dimensional phase-field lattice Boltzmann method that simulates Herschel-Bulkley fluids, which have both a yield stress and a power-law dependence on shear rate, interacting with a Newtonian fluid.

The model was verified and validated over a diverse set of rheological properties and flow conditions. Results for steady layered Poiseuille flow agreed closely with the analytical solution, and comparisons for the rise of a power-law fluid in a capillary tube also showed good agreement, while highlighting some sensitivity to initial conditions. A demonstration in a microfluidic junction showed how non-Newtonian rheology can change flow behaviour compared with purely Newtonian cases, and that small, smooth changes in rheological parameters can produce significant changes in behaviour.

**Authors:** [B.M. Hill](/people/b-hill/), [T.R. Mitchell](/people/t-mitchell/), [Ł. Łaniewski-Wołłk](/people/l-laniewski-wollk/), S.M. Aminossadati and [C.R. Leonardi](/people/c-leonardi/)

**Published in** *Computers and Mathematics with Applications* (2024). DOI: [10.1016/j.camwa.2024.10.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2024.10.035)
