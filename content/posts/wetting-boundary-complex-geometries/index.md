Simulating liquid-gas flow through rough fractures and other complex geometries requires boundary conditions that correctly represent wetting on curved surfaces. This study implemented and analysed two common wetting boundary conditions, the geometrical and surface-energy approaches, in the three-dimensional phase-field lattice Boltzmann method. It extends the geometrical approach to curved 3D surfaces and introduces an interpolation-based treatment that reduces the staircase approximation of curved boundaries, with an emphasis on simplicity and parallel computing efficiency.

The methods were validated on 3D benchmarks including droplet spreading on a sphere, capillary intrusion and droplet impact on a sphere, highlighting the differences between them. Two-phase flow through a synthetic rough fracture demonstrated the approach in a realistic geometry and was used to compare the computational performance of the methods.

**Authors:** [D. Sashko](/people/d-sashko/), [T.R. Mitchell](/people/t-mitchell/), [Ł. Łaniewski-Wołłk](/people/l-laniewski-wollk/) and [C.R. Leonardi](/people/c-leonardi/)

**Published in** *Computers and Mathematics with Applications* (2025). DOI: [10.1016/j.camwa.2025.03.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2025.03.014)
